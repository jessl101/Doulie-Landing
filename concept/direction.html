Doulie · Landing page · Creative direction

The Long Night

One night, told in hours. The page stops describing a sleep app and starts keeping a tired parent company from 11:40 PM to first light, in the palette Doulie already owns.

Treatment v1
3 September 2026
Companion prototype: Doulie, The Long Night

Concept

The idea in one line

"Someone's awake with you."

Every baby-sleep product sells the morning after: the rested parent, the sleeping infant, the chart trending up. Doulie's audience is not there yet. She is on the nursery floor at 2:47 AM with a phone at 4% brightness, and the last thing she wants is a dashboard.

So the site becomes the night itself. The scroll is the clock. Each section is an hour, with its own light, its own sky, and one thing Doulie does at that hour. The page opens at the worst moment (the third wake-up) and ends at dawn. Luxury here is not marble and gold leaf. It is the luxury of quiet: one light source, one line of type, one thing to try.

What "ultra-luxury" means for Doulie. Restraint, materials that feel touched by light, a camera that never hurries, and copy that speaks at a whisper. If a screen would feel loud in a dark bedroom, it is wrong for this brand.

What stays exactly as it is

  • The palette. Night navy, cream, moonlight lavender, gold, sage. No new hues.
  • The typeface pairing. Instrument Serif italic as the voice, Satoshi as the hand.
  • The wordmark with its three four-point sparks. Those sparks become the only shape language on the page.
  • The waitlist form posting to Supabase, the SEO metadata, the legal and medical disclaimers.

Art direction

Principles

One light source per hour

Moonlight (lavender, top right) at bedtime. The nightlight (gold, bottom left) at 2:47. Dawn (sage into gold, from below) at 6:10. Every glow, rim highlight and shadow on the page derives from whichever source is on. Two competing lights is the fastest way to make a dark page look like a template.

Type is the protagonist

The headline is the largest object on every screen and the props sit lower in the hierarchy, like furniture in a dark room. The italic serif reads as a voice, not a logo, so it carries every emotional line. Satoshi never goes above 20px.

Nothing is a card until it has to be

The current page has six cards, six stage cards, a join card and six FAQ rows. The concept keeps one glass surface per beat, and everything else sits directly on the sky. Border, blur and shadow are spent on the single object that represents the app at that hour.

The spark is the only ornament

The four-point star from the wordmark appears as nine gold sparks in the 3D field and one pinned to the corner of each glass prop. No icons in circles, no gradient blobs, no checkmarks, no emoji.

Left behind

  • Centred hero over a centred grid
  • Icon-in-a-box feature cards
  • Horizontal carousel with arrows and dots
  • Accordion FAQ as a primary section
  • Two identical forms in two identical cards
  • Purple-to-blue gradients, glassmorphism on everything

In their place

  • Left-anchored type with the scene breathing on the right
  • One working prop per hour, showing real app states
  • Stages become a quiet "at 5 months" line in the exchange, and a later toggle
  • FAQ as a plain list after the close
  • Form as a single frosted row: field, verb
  • One glass material, one gold hairline, one grain

Composition

Grid and rhythm

A twelve-column frame, but every beat uses it as 7 / 5: type on the wide side, the prop on the narrow side, and the split mirrors on each beat so the eye crosses the page like a slow pan. The close is the only centred beat, which is why it lands.

  • Content width 1180px. Lede measure 52ch. Beats are a full viewport tall so each hour gets its own frame.
  • Vertical rhythm comes from the beats themselves, not from section padding. There is no divider, no rule, no numbered marker between hours: the sky changes instead.
  • Eyebrow, headline, lede, then either the form or two feature notes. Never more than two features per hour. Six features across three hours.
  • Props are sized to their content and sit at the vertical centre of the beat, slightly below the headline's cap height, like an object on a nightstand.

On phones the grid collapses to one column, the prop drops under the copy, and tilt is switched off. The star field and the sky transitions are the mobile experience; they cost nothing in layout.

Materials

Textures and finishes

Night velvetThe ground. A vertical gradient from deep night to navy, so the top of every screen is darker than the bottom, like a room lit from a low lamp.
Frosted lavender glassThe one surface. Navy at 50–70% over a 22px backdrop blur, a one-pixel cream highlight along the top edge, and a diagonal sheen that picks up moonlight top-left and nightlight bottom-right.
Brushed gold hairlineEyebrow rules, the "now" line on the timeline, the spark. Always a gradient to transparent, never a solid stroke. Reads as a thread of light, not a border.
Film grainA fractal-noise layer at 5% over everything, stepping six frames a second. It makes the gradients feel photographed rather than rendered and hides banding on cheap displays.

A radial vignette darkens the outer 40% of the viewport by up to 55%. It is the cheapest way to make a flat screen feel like a room.

Light and depth

Atmosphere · camera

Lighting

Three sources, never all at once. Moonlight is a 900 × 620px lavender radial at 82% / −6%. The nightlight is a 700 × 520px gold radial at 6% / 96%, at 35% in the hero and 95% at the wake-up. Dawn is a 1400 × 520px sage-to-gold radial from the bottom centre, with the whole sky crossfading to first light over 0.9s.

Depth

The stars are a true 3D volume, not a parallax of flat layers: around a thousand points in a box 3400 × 2200 × 2500 units, projected through a 720px focal length. Size and brightness fall off with distance, and anything far from the 900-unit focal plane is drawn soft and wide, so near stars become bokeh and far stars become dust. Nine of them are the wordmark's gold four-point spark, slowly rotating.

Camera language

MoveAmountTrigger
Intro dolly520 units forward, eased over ≈ 6sPage load. The stars settle as the headline rises.

Cursor pan± 90 × 60 unitsPointer position, smoothed. Enough to feel the volume, never enough to notice the mouse.
Drift± 18 units, 57-second periodAlways on. The room breathes even when nothing else moves.
Prop tilt≤ 7° yaw, ≤ 6° pitchCursor over the prop, fine pointers only. Off on touch.

There are no zooms, no whip pans, no scroll-jacking. The user's scroll is always the user's scroll.

Motion

Timing and restraint

  • One easing. cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1), the same curve the production stylesheet already uses, at durations between 0.9s and 1.4s. Nothing snaps.
  • Everything is visible at rest. Type rises 28px and fades in as a beat enters, from a resting state that is already legible. The prop's data draws itself once: timeline segments scale from the left, bars grow from the baseline, messages arrive half a second apart.
  • Ambient motion is slow and small. Star twinkle on a 4–6s sine. Spark rotation at 0.08 rad/s. The clock dot breathes on a 4.2s cycle. Grain steps at 6 fps.
  • The clock changes with the hour. A 180ms fade out and in when the beat changes. It is the only thing on the page that ever "cuts".
  • Reduced motion is a still. With prefers-reduced-motion, the star field renders one frame, every reveal is already complete, grain and breathing stop. The page is fully readable as a photograph.

Build plan

Getting it onto douliesleep.com

The production site is static HTML with one stylesheet and one script, no build step. The concept fits that: the prototype is a single file with no dependencies, and everything it does is CSS, Canvas 2D and about 120 lines of vanilla script.

Phase 1 · The hero and the sky

  • Replace the CSS-only star field in index.html with the Canvas volume; keep the existing static radial-gradient stars as the no-JS fallback.
  • Move the hero to the 7 / 5 layout, single-row form, bedside clock. The Supabase submit in site.js is untouched; only the markup around the input changes.
  • Add the grain, vignette and sky planes. Confirm the page still ships under 250KB without the wordmark data URI (production loads the PNG normally).

Phase 2 · The hours

  • Recut the six feature cards into the three hour beats with two notes each; port the three props.
  • Fold the six-stage carousel into an "at 5 months" line in the exchange, and keep the stage select in the form. A per-age toggle in the exchange is a later option.
  • Move the FAQ below the close as a plain list, keep the JSON-LD and legal copy.

Guardrails

  • Body text contrast stays above 7:1 on the darkest sky; muted text above 4.5:1 on glass.
  • Star count scales with viewport area and caps at 1100; device pixel ratio capped at 2. Target 60fps on a three-year-old phone, and the frame loop stops entirely under reduced motion.
  • Every test id the current markup carries (hero-form, footer-form, appstore-badge) survives the recut.
